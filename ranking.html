How Rankings Work

A plain-English guide to the Points

←

Every player gets a score out of 1000 in each category — Batting, Bowling, All-rounder and Fielding. The leaderboard simply sorts everyone by those points. Here's what goes into them, in everyday terms.

Points are relative, not absolute. You're measured against the best player in the group for the selected format. That means your points can move even in a week you didn't play — if the top performer's numbers change, everyone else's scores shift around them.

🏏 Batting

Four ingredients are blended together. The percentage is how much each one matters:

30%
Quality — Batting Average Runs scored per time you got out. But a high average only counts fully if it's backed by enough runs. A "40 average" off just 40 runs (lots of not-outs) earns little credit — the same average built on 400 runs earns the full share. The credit you lose here is handed to Volume below.
25%
Intent — Strike Rate How fast you score — runs per 100 balls. Rewards aggressive scoring.
25%
Volume — Total Runs How many runs you've actually piled up across the season. Big contributors rank higher.
20%
Consistency — 30s & 50s Turning starts into meaningful scores. A fifty counts double a thirty.

Fairness check: if you've only batted a few innings, your score is scaled down so a couple of lucky knocks can't outrank the regulars who do it week after week.

🎯 Bowling

You must have bowled and taken at least one wicket to score any points. Then four ingredients are blended:

30%
Wickets Total wickets taken — the core job of a bowler.
25%
Economy Runs you give away per over. Fewer is better — tight bowling scores higher.
25%
Efficiency — Bowling Average Runs conceded per wicket. Fewer is better — striking often without leaking runs.
20%
Impact — 3-Wicket Hauls Match-turning spells where you ran through the batting.

Fairness check: bowled only a handful of overs? Your score is scaled down, so one cheap wicket in a single over can't shoot you to the top.

⭐ All-rounder

Your Batting points multiplied by your Bowling points. Because it's a multiplication, you need to be good at both — being brilliant at one and poor at the other won't get you far. You also need a minimum number of batting innings and bowling overs to qualify at all.

🧤 Fielding

The simplest one: total dismissals = Catches + Stumpings + Run-outs. Whoever has the most is ranked first.

🗂️ Overall, Turf & Ground

Each format tab is scored on its own, comparing only the players who played that format. Because fewer Turf and Ground matches are played, the qualification bar (minimum innings and overs) is lower on those tabs than on Overall.
